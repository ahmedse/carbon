# Scope 1 Emissions: Direct GHG Emissions

## Definition

Scope 1 emissions are direct greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from sources that are owned or controlled by the reporting organization. These are emissions for which the company has direct responsibility.

## Categories of Scope 1 Emissions

### 1. Stationary Combustion

Combustion of fuels in stationary equipment such as:
- **Boilers**: Used for heating, steam generation
- **Furnaces**: Industrial heating processes
- **Turbines**: Power generation
- **Heaters**: Space and process heating
- **Incinerators**: Waste treatment
- **Flares**: Disposal of volatile organic compounds

**Common Fuels:**
- Natural gas
- Diesel/fuel oil
- Coal
- Propane/LPG
- Biomass

**Calculation Method:**
```
Emissions = Fuel Consumed × Emission Factor
```

### 2. Mobile Combustion

Combustion of fuels in transportation equipment:

**Vehicle Types:**
- Company-owned cars and trucks
- Forklifts and warehouse equipment
- Ships and boats
- Aircraft
- Rail vehicles

**Fuel Types:**
- Gasoline/petrol
- Diesel
- Jet fuel
- Marine fuels
- Compressed natural gas (CNG)
- Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG)

**Calculation Approaches:**
1. **Fuel-based**: Emissions = Fuel × Emission Factor
2. **Distance-based**: Emissions = Distance × Vehicle Emission Factor

### 3. Process Emissions

GHG emissions from industrial processes (not combustion):

**Examples:**
- Cement production (calcination of limestone)
- Aluminum smelting
- Ammonia production
- Iron and steel production
- Chemical reactions in manufacturing
- Semiconductor manufacturing (PFCs, SF6)

### 4. Fugitive Emissions

Unintentional releases of GHGs:

**Common Sources:**
- **Refrigeration and Air Conditioning**: HFC and HCFC leaks
- **Industrial Equipment**: SF6 from electrical equipment
- **Oil and Gas Operations**: Methane leaks from pipelines
- **Coal Mining**: Methane releases
- **Landfills**: Methane from decomposition (if owned)

**Key Refrigerants and GWPs:**
| Refrigerant | GWP (100-year) |
|-------------|----------------|
| R-134a (HFC) | 1,430 |
| R-410A (HFC) | 2,088 |
| R-32 (HFC) | 675 |
| R-404A (HFC) | 3,922 |
| R-22 (HCFC) | 1,810 |

## Calculating Scope 1 Emissions

### Step 1: Identify Emission Sources
Map all direct emission sources within organizational boundaries.

### Step 2: Collect Activity Data
- Fuel purchase records
- Meter readings
- Equipment inventories
- Refrigerant purchase/disposal records

### Step 3: Select Emission Factors
Use appropriate factors from:
- IPCC Guidelines
- National inventories (EPA, DEFRA)
- Industry-specific factors

### Step 4: Calculate Emissions
For each source:
```
CO2e = Activity Data × Emission Factor × GWP
```

### Step 5: Aggregate and Report
Sum all sources by gas type and convert to CO2 equivalents.

## Best Practices for Scope 1 Accounting

1. **Maintain comprehensive fuel records** with dates, quantities, and fuel types
2. **Track refrigerant inventories** including purchases, disposals, and equipment changes
3. **Use mass balance** for process emissions where direct measurement isn't available
4. **Document calculation methods** and data sources for audit trail
5. **Apply appropriate Global Warming Potentials (GWPs)** - use AR5 or AR6 values consistently

## Common Scope 1 Emission Factors

### Natural Gas
- 0.185 kg CO2e per kWh (gross CV)
- 56.1 kg CO2e per therm
- 2.02 kg CO2 per cubic meter

### Diesel (Road)
- 2.68 kg CO2e per liter
- 10.14 kg CO2e per gallon
- 3.16 kg CO2e per kg

### Petrol/Gasoline
- 2.31 kg CO2e per liter
- 8.74 kg CO2e per gallon
- 3.17 kg CO2e per kg

### LPG
- 1.56 kg CO2e per liter
- 2.98 kg CO2e per kg

## Data Quality Requirements

- Primary data from meters and invoices preferred
- Secondary data from estimates and models acceptable where primary unavailable
- Document uncertainty levels
- Report data gaps transparently
