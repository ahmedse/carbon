# GHG Protocol Corporate Standard Overview

## Introduction to the GHG Protocol

The GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard (Corporate Standard) provides requirements and guidance for companies and other organizations preparing a corporate-level GHG emissions inventory. It was developed jointly by the World Resources Institute (WRI) and the World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD).

## Key Principles

The GHG Protocol is built on five fundamental principles:

### 1. Relevance
Ensure the GHG inventory appropriately reflects the GHG emissions of the company and serves the decision-making needs of users – both internal and external to the company.

### 2. Completeness
Account for and report on all GHG emission sources and activities within the chosen inventory boundary. Disclose and justify any specific exclusions.

### 3. Consistency
Use consistent methodologies to allow for meaningful comparisons of emissions over time. Transparently document any changes to the data, inventory boundary, methods, or any other relevant factors in the time series.

### 4. Transparency
Address all relevant issues in a factual and coherent manner, based on a clear audit trail. Disclose any relevant assumptions and make appropriate references to the accounting and calculation methodologies and data sources used.

### 5. Accuracy
Ensure that the quantification of GHG emissions is systematically neither over nor under actual emissions, as far as can be judged, and that uncertainties are reduced as far as practicable. Achieve sufficient accuracy to enable users to make decisions with reasonable assurance as to the integrity of the reported information.

## Scopes of Emissions

The GHG Protocol categorizes greenhouse gas emissions into three scopes:

### Scope 1: Direct Emissions
Emissions from sources that are owned or controlled by the organization:
- Stationary combustion (boilers, furnaces, turbines)
- Mobile combustion (company vehicles, trucks, aircraft)
- Process emissions (chemical processes, manufacturing)
- Fugitive emissions (refrigerant leaks, methane from pipelines)

### Scope 2: Indirect Energy Emissions
Emissions from the generation of purchased electricity, steam, heating, and cooling consumed by the organization:
- Purchased electricity
- Purchased steam
- Purchased heating
- Purchased cooling

### Scope 3: Other Indirect Emissions
All other indirect emissions that occur in the value chain:
- Upstream emissions (purchased goods, transportation, business travel)
- Downstream emissions (product use, end-of-life treatment)

## Organizational Boundaries

Companies can use either:

### Equity Share Approach
Account for GHG emissions according to the organization's equity share in operations. Under this approach, a company accounts for emissions based on its percentage ownership.

### Control Approach
Account for 100% of emissions from operations over which the organization has control:
- **Financial Control**: The organization has the ability to direct the financial and operating policies
- **Operational Control**: The organization has the full authority to introduce and implement operating policies

## Base Year Selection

Organizations should select and report a performance datum called the "base year" for tracking emissions over time:
- Should be the earliest relevant point in time for which reliable data is available
- Emissions profile should be representative of typical operations
- Must be recalculated when significant structural changes occur

## Reporting Requirements

A complete GHG inventory should include:
1. Description of the company and inventory boundary
2. Description of the reporting period
3. Total GHG emissions by scope
4. Reporting methodology and emission factors used
5. Description of any exclusions or recalculations
6. Statement of data quality and verification
