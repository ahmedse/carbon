## Overview

Grid emission factors represent the carbon intensity of electricity generation. They vary significantly by country based on the energy mix.

## Time-of-Use Considerations

Grid intensity varies by:
- **Time of day**: Lower at night (less demand)
- **Season**: Heating/cooling demand affects mix
- **Weather**: Renewables depend on sun/wind

### Example: UK Grid Intensity Variation
| Time Period | Typical Range (kg CO2e/kWh) |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| Night (11pm-6am) | 0.150-0.200 |
| Morning peak (7-9am) | 0.250-0.300 |
| Midday | 0.180-0.250 |
| Evening peak (5-8pm) | 0.280-0.350 |

## Residual Mix vs Grid Average

### Location-Based (Grid Average)
- Average emissions of all generators on the grid
- Used when no contractual instruments are in place

### Market-Based (Residual Mix)
- Excludes emissions claimed by certificate holders
- Typically higher than grid average
- Used when not purchasing renewable certificates

### Example Comparison
| Country | Grid Average | Residual Mix |
|---------|--------------|--------------|
| UK | 0.233 | 0.284 |
| Germany | 0.366 | 0.432 |
| Netherlands | 0.310 | 0.415 |
| USA | 0.417 | 0.460 |

## Best Practices

1. **Use location-specific factors** where available
2. **Update annually** - factors change as grids decarbonize
3. **Consider time-of-use** for demand-response reporting
4. **Report dual Scope 2** (location and market-based)
5. **Document data sources** and vintages
