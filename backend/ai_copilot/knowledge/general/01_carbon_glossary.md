# Carbon Footprint Glossary

## A

### Absolute Emissions
Total greenhouse gas emissions expressed in metric tonnes of CO2 equivalent (tCO2e), without relation to any other metric like revenue or production.

### Additionality
The requirement that emission reductions from a carbon offset project would not have occurred without the incentive provided by carbon finance. A key quality criterion for carbon credits.

### Anthropogenic Emissions
Greenhouse gas emissions resulting from human activities, as opposed to natural sources. Includes emissions from burning fossil fuels, deforestation, agriculture, and industrial processes.

### Avoided Emissions
Emissions that would have occurred under a baseline scenario but were prevented through specific actions or projects. Used in offset calculations.

## B

### Base Year
A historic datum (year) against which an organization's emissions are tracked over time. Should be representative of typical operations and have reliable data.

### Baseline Scenario
A reference case representing what would have occurred in the absence of a mitigation activity. Used to measure emission reductions.

### Biogenic CO2
Carbon dioxide released from the combustion or decomposition of biologically based materials. Often reported separately from fossil fuel emissions.

### Boundary (Organizational)
The scope of operations and entities included in a GHG inventory, determined by equity share or control approach.

### Boundary (Operational)
The categorization of direct and indirect emissions sources within the organizational boundary.

## C

### Carbon Credit
A tradeable certificate representing one metric tonne of CO2 equivalent either reduced or removed from the atmosphere.

### Carbon Dioxide Equivalent (CO2e)
A metric measure used to compare emissions from various greenhouse gases based on their global warming potential.

### Carbon Footprint
The total greenhouse gas emissions caused directly and indirectly by an individual, organization, event, or product.

### Carbon Intensity
A ratio metric expressing emissions relative to a business metric such as revenue, production, or floor area (e.g., tCO2e per $M revenue).

### Carbon Neutral
Achieving a balance between emitting carbon and absorbing carbon from the atmosphere through offsets or removals. Net emissions equal zero.

### Carbon Sink
A natural or artificial reservoir that accumulates and stores carbon dioxide, such as forests, oceans, or soil.

### Certified Emission Reduction (CER)
Carbon credits issued under the Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) of the Kyoto Protocol.

### Climate Positive
Going beyond carbon neutral by removing additional carbon dioxide from the atmosphere beyond emissions generated.

### CO2
Carbon dioxide, the most common greenhouse gas from human activities.

### Combustion Emissions
GHG emissions from the burning of fuels, including stationary (boilers) and mobile (vehicles) sources.

## D

### Decarbonization
The process of reducing carbon intensity and eventually eliminating carbon dioxide emissions from processes, products, or energy systems.

### Direct Emissions
GHG emissions from sources owned or controlled by the organization. See Scope 1.

### Double Counting
When a single GHG emission reduction or removal is counted more than once toward climate targets.

## E

### Emission Factor
A coefficient that relates activity data to GHG emissions. Expressed as emissions per unit of activity (e.g., kg CO2e per kWh).

### Energy Attribute Certificate (EAC)
A generic term for certificates that convey environmental attributes of energy generation, including RECs and GOs.

## F

### Financed Emissions
Indirect GHG emissions associated with a financial institution's lending and investment portfolio.

### Fugitive Emissions
Unintentional releases of GHGs, such as refrigerant leaks or methane from pipelines. Part of Scope 1.

## G

### Global Warming Potential (GWP)
A measure of how much energy a greenhouse gas will absorb over a given period relative to carbon dioxide. Used to convert gases to CO2 equivalents.

### Greenhouse Gas (GHG)
Atmospheric gases that absorb and emit infrared radiation, causing the greenhouse effect. Includes CO2, CH4, N2O, HFCs, PFCs, SF6, and NF3.

### Green Premium
The additional cost of choosing a low-carbon technology or product over a conventional alternative.

### Greenwashing
Making misleading claims about environmental performance or the environmental benefits of a product or service.

## H

### HFCs (Hydrofluorocarbons)
Synthetic greenhouse gases commonly used in refrigeration and air conditioning. High global warming potential.

## I

### Indirect Emissions
GHG emissions that occur as a consequence of activities from sources not owned or controlled by the organization. See Scopes 2 and 3.

### Intensity Target
An emission reduction target defined as a ratio of emissions to a business metric, allowing for growth while reducing intensity.

### IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change)
The United Nations body assessing climate change science, providing guidelines for national GHG inventories.

## L

### Leakage
When emission reductions in one area lead to increased emissions in another area, reducing the net benefit.

### Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)
A method for evaluating the environmental impacts of a product or service throughout its entire life cycle, from raw materials to disposal.

### Location-Based Method
Scope 2 accounting method reflecting the average emissions intensity of the grid where energy consumption occurs.

## M

### Market-Based Method
Scope 2 accounting method reflecting emissions from electricity that organizations have purposefully chosen through contractual instruments.

### Materiality
The significance of an emission source in the context of the overall inventory. Material sources typically require more accurate measurement.

### Methane (CH4)
A potent greenhouse gas with a GWP of approximately 28-30 over 100 years. Main sources include agriculture, fossil fuels, and waste.

### Mitigation
Actions to reduce or prevent GHG emissions, such as improving energy efficiency or switching to renewable energy.

## N

### Net Zero
Achieving a balance between GHG emissions produced and removed from the atmosphere. Requires deep emission reductions (typically 90%+) and neutralizing residual emissions with removals.

### Nitrous Oxide (N2O)
A greenhouse gas with a GWP of approximately 265-273 over 100 years. Main sources include agriculture and industrial processes.

## O

### Offset
See Carbon Offset. A mechanism to compensate for emissions by funding equivalent carbon dioxide savings elsewhere.

### Operational Control
One approach to setting organizational boundaries where the organization accounts for 100% of emissions from operations it controls.

## P

### Paris Agreement
International treaty adopted in 2015 aiming to limit global warming to 1.5-2°C above pre-industrial levels.

### Permanence
The requirement that carbon storage or emission reductions are durable over time. A key quality criterion for carbon credits.

### Power Purchase Agreement (PPA)
A contract between electricity buyer and generator, often used for renewable energy procurement.

### Process Emissions
GHG emissions from industrial processes other than combustion, such as chemical reactions in cement production.

## R

### Radiative Forcing
The change in energy flux in the atmosphere caused by natural or human factors. Used in climate models.

### REDD+
Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation. A framework for forest carbon projects.

### Renewable Energy Certificate (REC)
A market-based instrument representing the environmental attributes of renewable electricity generation.

### Residual Emissions
Emissions remaining after all feasible reduction measures have been implemented. Typically neutralized with carbon removal for net-zero.

### Residual Mix
The mix of electricity resources remaining after contractual instruments have been claimed. Used for market-based Scope 2.

## S

### Science-Based Target (SBT)
An emission reduction target aligned with the level of decarbonization required by climate science to meet Paris Agreement goals.

### Scope 1
Direct GHG emissions from sources owned or controlled by the organization.

### Scope 2
Indirect GHG emissions from purchased electricity, steam, heating, and cooling.

### Scope 3
All other indirect emissions in the value chain, including upstream and downstream activities.

### Sequestration
The capture and long-term storage of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.

### SF6 (Sulfur Hexafluoride)
A potent greenhouse gas used in electrical equipment. Has the highest GWP of all GHGs.

## T

### TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures)
Framework for climate-related financial disclosures covering governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics.

### Tonne of CO2 Equivalent (tCO2e)
Standard unit for measuring carbon footprints. One metric tonne = 1,000 kg.

### Transition Plan
A strategy and roadmap for shifting to a low-carbon business model, including targets, actions, and timelines.

### Transition Risk
Financial risks associated with transitioning to a low-carbon economy, including policy, technology, and market changes.

## U

### Upstream Emissions
Indirect emissions that occur in the supply chain before the organization's operations.

## V

### Value Chain
The full range of activities needed to create a product or service, including suppliers, operations, and customers.

### Verification
Third-party assessment of a GHG inventory to ensure accuracy and conformance with standards.

### Voluntary Carbon Market
A market where carbon credits are bought and sold by organizations seeking to offset emissions voluntarily.

## W

### Well-to-Tank (WTT)
Emissions associated with extracting, refining, and transporting fuels before use. Part of Scope 3.

### Well-to-Wheel (WTW)
Total emissions from fuel extraction through vehicle use. Combines WTT and tank-to-wheel emissions.

## Z

### Zero Carbon
A process, product, or organization that produces no carbon dioxide emissions.

### Zero Emission Vehicle (ZEV)
A vehicle that produces no tailpipe emissions, such as battery electric or hydrogen fuel cell vehicles.
