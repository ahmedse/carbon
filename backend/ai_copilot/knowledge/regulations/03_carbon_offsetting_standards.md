# Carbon Offsetting and Neutrality Standards

## Overview

Carbon offsetting involves compensating for emissions by funding equivalent carbon dioxide savings elsewhere. This document covers standards, best practices, and controversies around offsetting.

## Key Definitions

### Carbon Neutral
Achieving net-zero carbon emissions by balancing emissions with carbon removal or offset purchases.

### Net Zero
Reducing emissions to the maximum extent possible and neutralizing residual emissions with permanent carbon removal.

### Carbon Offset
A reduction or removal of emissions made to compensate for emissions elsewhere.

### Carbon Credit
A tradeable instrument representing one metric tonne of CO2 equivalent (tCO2e) reduced or removed.

## Offset Quality Criteria

For offsets to have genuine climate impact, they must meet key criteria:

### 1. Additionality
The emission reduction would not have occurred without the offset project.

**Tests:**
- Financial additionality (project wouldn't be viable without carbon revenue)
- Regulatory additionality (not required by law)
- Barrier analysis (overcomes specific obstacles)

### 2. Permanence
The carbon reduction or storage is durable over time.

**Considerations:**
- Forest projects risk reversal (fires, disease)
- Geological storage more permanent
- Insurance and buffer pools mitigate reversal risk

### 3. Measurability (Quantification)
Emissions reductions can be accurately measured and verified.

**Requirements:**
- Robust methodology
- Conservative baseline
- Regular monitoring
- Third-party verification

### 4. Verification
Independent third-party assessment of claimed reductions.

**Verification Bodies:**
- Verra (VCS)
- Gold Standard
- American Carbon Registry
- Climate Action Reserve

### 5. No Leakage
Project doesn't simply shift emissions elsewhere.

**Example:** Protecting one forest shouldn't cause deforestation in another.

### 6. No Double Counting
The same reduction is not claimed multiple times.

**Mechanisms:**
- Registry tracking
- Corresponding adjustments (Article 6)
- Retirement requirements

## Major Offset Standards

### Verified Carbon Standard (VCS) - Verra

**Market Share:** Largest voluntary carbon market standard

**Project Types:**
- Renewable energy
- Forest conservation (REDD+)
- Afforestation/reforestation
- Blue carbon (mangroves, seagrass)
- Agriculture and land use

**Registry:** Verra Registry

### Gold Standard

**Focus:** High-quality projects with sustainable development co-benefits

**Project Types:**
- Clean cookstoves
- Renewable energy
- Forest restoration
- Energy efficiency

**Requirements:**
- UN SDG contributions
- Stakeholder consultation
- Robust MRV

### American Carbon Registry (ACR)

**Focus:** North American projects

**Project Types:**
- Forestry
- Livestock methane
- Landfill gas
- Clean energy

### Climate Action Reserve (CAR)

**Focus:** North American compliance and voluntary markets

**Project Types:**
- Forestry
- Livestock
- Organic waste
- Coal mine methane

### Plan Vivo

**Focus:** Smallholder and community land use projects

**Strengths:**
- Community benefits
- Biodiversity protection
- Long-term approach

## Offset Project Categories

### Avoidance/Reduction Projects

**Renewable Energy:**
- Wind farms
- Solar installations
- Small-scale hydro
- Biomass energy

*Note: Additionality increasingly questioned as renewables become cost-competitive*

**Energy Efficiency:**
- Industrial efficiency
- Building retrofits
- Clean cookstoves

**Methane Destruction:**
- Landfill gas capture
- Agricultural methane
- Coal mine methane

### Nature-Based Solutions (NBS)

**Forest Conservation (REDD+):**
- Avoid deforestation
- Reduce forest degradation
- Sustainable forest management

**Afforestation/Reforestation:**
- Planting new forests
- Restoring degraded land
- Agroforestry

**Blue Carbon:**
- Mangrove restoration
- Seagrass protection
- Coastal wetlands

**Soil Carbon:**
- Regenerative agriculture
- Grassland management
- Biochar application

### Technology-Based Removal

**Direct Air Capture (DAC):**
- Captures CO2 directly from atmosphere
- High permanence (geological storage)
- Currently expensive ($300-600/tonne)

**Bioenergy with Carbon Capture (BECCS):**
- Biomass energy with carbon storage
- Net negative if sustainably sourced
- Scalability challenges

**Enhanced Weathering:**
- Accelerated mineral carbonation
- Spreading silicate rocks on land
- Long-term removal

## Corporate Use of Offsets

### Mitigation Hierarchy

**Best practice order:**
1. **Avoid** emissions where possible
2. **Reduce** emissions through efficiency
3. **Substitute** with low-carbon alternatives
4. **Compensate** residual emissions

### Current Guidance

**SBTi Net-Zero Standard:**
- Offsets don't count toward target achievement
- Only for neutralizing residual emissions after 90% reduction
- Must use permanent carbon removals

**Oxford Principles for Net Zero:**
1. Prioritize reducing own emissions
2. Shift to carbon removal offsets over time
3. Shift to long-lived storage over time
4. Support development of net-zero aligned offsets

### Disclosure Best Practices

- Separate reduction claims from offset claims
- Disclose offset quantity, type, standard, and registry
- Describe offset selection criteria
- Report vintage and retirement details

## Controversies and Challenges

### Common Criticisms

**Over-crediting:**
- Baselines may be inflated
- Additionality hard to prove
- Some projects generate credits beyond real reductions

**Permanence Risks:**
- Forest fires reversing sequestration
- Land-use changes
- Inadequate buffer pools

**Social Concerns:**
- Land rights of indigenous communities
- "Carbon colonialism" accusations
- Benefit sharing inequity

### Recent Developments

**Integrity Council for Voluntary Carbon Markets (ICVCM):**
- Core Carbon Principles (CCPs)
- Raising quality standards
- Assessment framework for methodologies

**Voluntary Carbon Markets Integrity Initiative (VCMI):**
- Claims Code of Practice
- Guidance for corporate use
- Tiered claims framework

## Article 6 of the Paris Agreement

### Corresponding Adjustments

When offsets are used internationally:
- Host country must adjust NDC
- Prevents double counting
- Required for Paris-aligned claims

### Article 6.2 (Bilateral)
- Cooperative approaches between countries
- Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs)

### Article 6.4 (UN Mechanism)
- Successor to Clean Development Mechanism
- UN-supervised crediting mechanism
- Share of proceeds for adaptation

## Best Practices for Buyers

1. **Prioritize emission reductions** before offsetting
2. **Use quality criteria** to screen projects
3. **Diversify project types** to manage risk
4. **Prefer removal over avoidance** for long-term strategy
5. **Verify through registries** - ensure credits are retired
6. **Disclose transparently** - be clear about offset use
7. **Engage with developers** - understand project impacts
8. **Plan for price increases** as demand grows
