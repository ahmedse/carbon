1. Executive Summary
GOFSCO should operate its four stores as one integrated inventory network rather than four independent
warehouses. Ahmadi, Coiled Tubing, Drilling and PCP will retain their physical responsibility for custody and
service to operations, while item definitions, transaction rules, approval limits, valuation, document control and
performance reporting remain standardized.
The recommended ERP model uses a single item master and separates stock by site, warehouse, zone, rack, shelf
and bin. Each quantity also carries a stock status such as available, reserved, inspection, quarantine, damaged,
expired, customer-owned, vendor-owned or scrap. This structure allows management to see exactly what GOFSCO
owns, where it is, whether it can be used, which job or department needs it and what action is pending.
Management outcome: The system should reduce stockouts, emergency purchases, duplicate buying,
undocumented issues, expired chemicals, unexplained variances and slow-moving inventory while
improving job readiness, cost allocation, auditability and cash control.
1.1 Current-state problems requiring correction
The current Sage 300 process depends heavily on users navigating separate screens, manually entering
information from hardcopy forms, printing reports and then exporting or reorganizing the results in Excel. The
screenshots and process notes supplied for item creation, purchase orders, receiving, internal usage/MIV, physical
inventory, MIV reporting, MRV reporting and aged inventory confirm that the present process is functional but
fragmented and outdated for a four-store operation.

• Errors can pass from hardcopy into Sage and then into Excel without automated validation.
• Stores spends time preparing and cleaning reports instead of controlling stock and supporting operations.
• Management cannot depend on a single live view of available, reserved, quarantined, expired and in-transit

• Urgent requirements may cause verbal issues or late paperwork, increasing the backlog and weakening job-

• The company may repurchase material already held at another store because network availability is difficult

• Audit evidence is spread across paper, Sage and separate Excel files, making transaction reconstruction
slower and less reliable.
1.4 Target operating principles
• ERP first: every approved stock movement is recorded at the point of activity using barcode or QR scanning
wherever practical.
• No document, no movement: goods may not be received, issued, returned, transferred, adjusted or
disposed without the required reference and approval.
• One item, one code: duplicate item records are prevented through centralized item-master governance.
• Location accuracy: every item is assigned to a controlled location down to bin level, with clear labels and no
informal storage areas.
• Stock status is separate from physical location: quarantined or damaged material remains visible but cannot
be issued.
• Safety overrides convenience: chemical compatibility, SDS requirements, ventilation, containment and
emergency arrangements govern storage.
• Segregation of duties: requesting, approving, receiving, recording, counting and adjusting are separated to
the extent practical.
• Exception-driven management: dashboards highlight shortages, overdue approvals, expiry, excess stock,
negative stock attempts and count variances.

2.2 Objectives
• Provide reliable stock availability for operations without carrying uncontrolled excess.
• Create seamless, auditable workflows from demand planning through purchase, receipt, storage, issue,
return and disposal.
• Establish clear ownership and accountability at each store.
• Provide management with real-time visibility of stock quantity, value, condition, expiry, demand and
movement.
• Integrate Stores with Operations, Procurement, Finance, HSE, Maintenance and Asset Management.
• Create a scalable foundation for ERP implementation, barcode scanning and future automation.
2.3 Boundaries
Inventory consists of consumables, spare parts, chemicals and controlled operational material intended for issue
or use. Capital assets and serialized equipment remain linked to the Asset Register, although their receipt, custody
transfer and storage location may pass through the Stores process. Customer-owned, rental, consignment and
vendor-owned material must be clearly identified and must never be valued as GOFSCO-owned inventory.

Planning parameters must be set per item and warehouse. A critical CT seal may need minimum stock at CTU,

Safety stock Demand variability and supply uncertainty during lead time, increased for critical items.

Maximum stock Reorder point + planned order cycle quantity, constrained by shelf life, space and budget.

Available projected stock On hand + open PO + approved inbound transfer - reservations - expected demand.

• Operations submits a rolling 12-month demand forecast, with a firm near-term window and indicative later
months.
• Job-specific material is linked to job number, customer, well/rig/equipment and planned date.
• Maintenance spares are linked to equipment BOM and preventive-maintenance schedules.
• Stores compares demand against on-hand, reserved, open purchase orders, transfers and historical usage.
• Supply Chain receives a consolidated replenishment proposal to prevent duplicated orders across
departments.
• Forecast accuracy and emergency purchase frequency are reviewed monthly with Operations.
6.4 Excess, slow-moving and obsolete stock
The ERP should calculate months since last issue and months of cover. A quarterly review board determines
whether material will be retained, transferred, returned to vendor, used as an approved substitute, sold, repaired,
donated where permitted or scrapped. No item is written off only because it has not moved; technical criticality
and equipment life must be considered.

• Backdating is restricted to authorized Finance/Inventory Control users and requires a reason.
• ERP must prevent negative stock and issuing blocked, expired or reserved material.
• Manual spreadsheets may support analysis but must not become an alternative stock ledger.
• System downtime transactions use pre-numbered controlled forms and are entered immediately after
restoration with supervisor review.

• Electronic signatures or authenticated approvals with delegated authority and absence cover.

• Interface controls and reconciliation between inventory, procurement, finance, maintenance and assets.

15.1 Critical cutover requirements
• No migration of unidentified or duplicate stock into available inventory.
• Opening quantity and value reconciled and approved by Stores and Finance.
• All locations labelled and physically ready before loading stock.
• Users trained and access tested before go-live.
• Open POs, reservations, returns, transfers and inspection items agreed at cutover.
• Daily command-center review during stabilization, with ownership and due date for every issue.
• Legacy spreadsheets changed to read-only archive after acceptance; ERP becomes the official ledger.
15.2 Training
Training must be role-based and practical. Storekeepers complete receiving, put-away, picking, issue, return,
transfer, counting and chemical scenarios. Requesters and approvers practice correct item selection, job/cost-
center coding and mobile approvals. Finance, HSE and administrators receive dedicated control and reporting
training. Competence is confirmed by supervised transactions, not attendance alone.

18. Conclusion
A seamless GOFSCO stores operation depends less on the ERP brand than on disciplined data, clear ownership and
mandatory workflows. The four stores should operate as one network with standardized rules while preserving
local custody and operational responsiveness. When every item has one identity, every quantity has one known
location and status, and every movement has an approved transaction, the ERP can provide reliable stock
availability, cost visibility and audit control.
The recommended approach should be implemented in controlled phases, beginning with physical and data
accuracy. This prevents the company from transferring existing inconsistencies into a new system and creates a
practical foundation for automation, performance management and future growth.
