Phase 1 – Operational Improvement Framework
Prepared by:
Ali Jafarian

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report outlines a structured operational improvement plan for GOFSCO stores with the
objective of enhancing inventory control, improving operational efficiency, strengthening
asset tracking, and standardizing store procedures across all departments.
The initial implementation phase will focus on the Ahmadi Main Store, followed by gradual
standardization across Drilling, Coiled Tubing (CT), and PCP stores.
The proposed framework introduces inventory classification systems, movement tracking,
KPI monitoring, reconciliation procedures, asset verification controls, and centralized
consumable management to establish a controlled and efficient inventory environment.
2. OBJECTIVES
• Improve inventory accuracy and traceability
• Establish standardized inventory control procedures
• Reduce discrepancies and undocumented stock movements
• Ensure operational readiness through controlled stock levels
• Improve consumable and spare parts management
• Strengthen asset verification and tracking procedures
• Enhance reporting visibility for management decision-making
3. GENERAL STORE IMPLEMENTATION
A centralized General Store is proposed to manage all common consumables and locally
sourced operational items shared between departments.
The General Store will primarily manage:

• Oils and lubricants
• Tires
• Truck spare parts
• Common consumables
• Frequently used operational materials
The purpose of the General Store is to:
• Reduce duplicate stocking across departments
• Improve stock visibility and control
• Standardize consumable management
• Optimize procurement and inventory utilization
4. INVENTORY CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM
A. Criticality Classification
• Critical (A-Class): Items directly affecting operations where stock-out may halt
activities
• Operational (B-Class): Important items with manageable short-term replacement
options
• Non-Critical (C-Class): Items with low operational impact
B. Movement Classification
• Fast Moving Items
• Slow Moving Items
• Dead Stock
Movement classification will be based on frequency of issuance, last issue date, and
historical consumption trends.
5. MINIMUM & MAXIMUM STOCK CONTROL
Minimum and maximum stock levels will be established for consumables, spare parts, and
operational materials to ensure operational continuity and improved procurement
planning.
6. SCRAP MANAGEMENT SYSTEM
A dedicated Scrap Disposal Zone will be implemented within each yard/store location with
physical segregation, tagging, and monthly review procedures.

=== Page 3 ===
7. NON-SAGE MATERIAL CONTROL
Each yard/store will establish a segregated holding area for items currently not listed
within the Sage system to improve safeguarding, tracking, and accountability.
8. INVENTORY TRACKING & CONTROL
A. Real-Time Updating
All inventory transactions (MIV/MRV) shall be recorded on the same day of movement.
B. Weekly Reconciliation
Weekly reconciliation will compare physical inventory against system records.
C. Monthly Audit
Monthly audits will verify quantities, documentation compliance, stock condition, and
slow/dead stock identification.
9. MONTHLY STORE REPORTING
Each storekeeper will prepare a monthly operational report including issued items,
received items, monthly consumption, slow-moving items, dead stock observations, and
discrepancies.
10. DATA STRUCTURE ENHANCEMENT
The inventory database structure will be enhanced to include:
• Last Issue Date
• Last Received Date
• Item Status (Active / Slow Moving / Dead Stock)
11. ASSET VERIFICATION CONTROL
Storekeepers will support physical asset verification, location confirmation, and
identification of missing or unregistered assets to improve accountability.
